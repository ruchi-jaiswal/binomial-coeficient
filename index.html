Origins

What is Meru-Prastāra?

Meru-Prastāra, Sanskrit for "expansion of the mountain," is a method for tabulating combinations described by the Indian scholar Pingala around the 2nd century BCE in his treatise on Sanskrit prosody, the Chandaḥśāstra. Centuries later, the commentator Halāyudha (10th century CE) explained the method in full: to build a row, place a 1 at each end, and let every number between them be the sum of the two numbers directly above it. Layered row upon row, the numbers form the shape of a mountain — hence the name.

That simple addition rule produces exactly the same array of numbers known in the West as Pascal's Triangle, named after Blaise Pascal, who described it in 1653 — nearly two thousand years after Pingala. Row n of the triangle holds the coefficients of the binomial expansion (a + b)n, and the entry at position r in that row is precisely the binomial coefficient C(n, r): the number of ways to choose r items from a set of n.

About

About this project

Purpose

A small educational tool that calculates binomial coefficients and shows exactly how the answer is reached — no black boxes, every step is visible.

How it works

The formula C(n, r) = n! ÷ [r! × (n − r)!] is computed step by step, and the same values are cross-checked against Meru-Prastāra's additive staircase.

Runs anywhere

Built with plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. Everything runs in your browser — no server, database, or account needed.
